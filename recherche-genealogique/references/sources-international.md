# International entry points

Contents: 1. How to use this page · 2. Entry points by country or region · 3. Cross-cutting resources · 4. Adapting the method to another country · 5. Credits

Written in English on purpose so that users outside France get usable starting points. **Answer in the user's language**; keep place names, archive names and quoted passages in their original form and translate them for the user.

## 1. How to use this page

- These are **leads, not verified facts about each site**. They were collected from other open projects (see § 5), not tested in a research session. Websites move: if an address fails, search the site's name. Availability, prices and access rules change.
- Provenance codes: **[AG]** = country guide actually read in the *autoresearch-genealogy* project (addresses as written there, last checked by its author on 2026-05-19) · **[AGi]** = only the index of that project, names without addresses · **[GA]** = the *Genealogy Research Agent* gist · **[EV]** = the *gramps-evidence-mcp* README.
- The evidence rules of this skill (levels A/B/C, named sources, copy don't recompose) apply in every country. Only the sources change.

## 3. Cross-cutting resources

- **FamilySearch** (free with an account): indexes and images worldwide [AG][GA]. **Ancestry**, **MyHeritage**, **Geneanet**: paid or partly paid trees and records [GA].
- **Find a Grave**, **BillionGraves**: burial records and photos [GA]. **Forebears.io**: surname distribution [GA]. **Cognomix.it**: Italian surname maps [GA].
- **Transcription and reading** [EV][GA]: Transkribus (handwriting), Library of Congress and Internet Archive (source documents), and local tools such as Tesseract, ocrmypdf, EasyOCR (printed text OCR) and Whisper (audio from relatives). Ask the user before sending any document to an online service.
- **Evidence-first data formats** (useful when exchanging data): GEDCOM 5.5.1 (this skill's import and export), GEDCOM X (FamilySearch), Gramps XML.

## 4. Adapting the method to another country

For a new country, answer these questions first, then fill the table above for the case at hand:
1. When did **civil registration** start, and who holds the records (state, county, church, archives)?
2. In which **language and script** are the records? Who can help read them?
3. Which **censuses**, military, immigration or land records exist, and are they indexed?
4. What are the **access rules** for recent records, and which **privacy law** applies (GDPR in the EU; elsewhere, check local rules)? Living people stay masked in shared documents everywhere.
5. How do **names** work (patronymics, spelling variants, name changes at migration)? Try variants before concluding "not found".
6. Which **calendar** was in force (Revolutionary calendar in France 1793-1805; the Julian-Gregorian switch occurred at different dates)?
7. **Inheritance law differs**: `recherche-heritiers.md` and `devolution.py` apply **French law only**. For another country, use the method (proofs, privacy, contact through the notary or equivalent) but not the legal order of heirs; ask a local professional.
